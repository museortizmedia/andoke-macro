# ORIGIN OF THE MAP OF COLOMBIA

Before you continue, stop for a moment. What you see in front of you began many years ago… with a piece of chalk and a mountain.

When Andoke first began, everything took place on a piece of land measuring just one thousand square meters. It was home to our house, the first butterfly house, the laboratory, and a small auditorium. María del Carmen then designed a program where children could learn about butterflies and nature through hands-on experiences. And schools began to visit. More and more of them. Until something wonderful happened: we outgrew the space.

So we acquired this neighboring piece of land: a hillside of approximately two thousand square meters. And Alejandro had an idea: What if, instead of learning about Colombia’s geography by looking at a map… children could actually step inside one? Walk among its mountain ranges. Follow its rivers. Discover where the water begins. Experience how the landscape changes. And above all, get to know the country so they could learn to love it.

The idea didn’t come out of nowhere. Alejandro had grown up surrounded by maps. His father, Manuel del Llano Buenaventura, a scientist and academic, had drawn a map of Colombia together with his wife, Luz Helena Restrepo. They later donated it to the Agustín Codazzi Geographic Institute. Alejandro remembers the living room of his childhood home filled with maps. Decades later, that boy who had learned to see Colombia on paper decided to do something different: take it off the page.

In 2006, he used chalk to trace the outline of the country directly onto this hillside. Then the mountain ranges began to take shape. With the help of neighbors, he dragged large stones across the hillside, shaped the terrain, built terraces, and created rivers, lagoons, and mountains. Then came the plants, the animals, and elements that tell the stories of the cultures that have inhabited these territories.

Little by little, that hillside began to become a country. And we are still building it today. Because we discovered something: a country like Colombia can never be fully told. There is always another landscape, another species, another culture, or another story that deserves a place here.

What you see in front of you is more than just a model. It is a Colombia you can walk through. Look. Listen. Feel the landscape change beneath your feet. We don’t want you to memorize Colombia. We want you to experience it.

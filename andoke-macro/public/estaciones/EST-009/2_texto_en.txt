# UPPER TERRACE

When you reach the upper terrace, stop for a moment. Before you look… listen.

Now, look around you. Here, you’ll probably find several butterflies feeding. Slowly approach the feeding stations. Do you see that small structure they unroll to feed? It’s called the proboscis, a flexible, tube-like mouthpart that butterflies use to drink liquids. Some butterflies get their energy from flower nectar; others also feed on the juices of ripe or decomposing fruit, like the fruit you can see here.

Now, look up. From this point, you can see the butterfly house from a different perspective. The plants. The water. The light. The humidity. The fruit. And the butterflies moving among them.

Perhaps from up here, we can understand something that wasn’t so obvious below: to protect a butterfly, it isn’t enough to protect the butterfly itself. We must protect the place that makes its life possible. The plants that provide food for the caterpillars. The flowers where butterflies find nectar. The water, shelter, and conditions they need to complete their life cycle. Everything is connected.

And we don’t need a butterfly house to help them. A garden, a balcony, or even a small space with the right plants can provide food and shelter. We can also avoid the unnecessary use of insecticides. And the next time you find a caterpillar eating a leaf… before you remove it, stop for a second and ask yourself: What might it become?

Before you leave, take one last look at the butterfly house from up here. Don’t look for anything in particular. Just look.

When you’re ready… continue your journey.

# HUMMINGBIRD AUDITORIUM

When you reach the auditorium, take a look at the gardens surrounding it. You may catch a glimpse of one of their fastest visitors while you’re here.

Hummingbirds are drawn to the flowers, searching for the nectar that gives them energy. But while they feed, something happens that we often don’t notice. As a hummingbird visits a flower, tiny grains of pollen can cling to its body. When it moves on to the next flower, it carries that pollen with it, helping the plants reproduce. So, while the hummingbird searches for food, the garden also finds a way to continue.

And there is something extraordinary about these birds: they can hover in midair, and they are the only birds capable of sustained backward flight. If one appears during your visit, take a moment to watch it. You may be witnessing one of nature’s most beautiful relationships: a flower feeding a bird… and a bird helping the garden continue to bloom.

Now, follow the path downhill toward the laboratory.

# THE PACIFIC AND CHOCÓ BIOGEOGRAPHIC REGION

Keep walking. Can you feel that something has changed? Moisture-laden air arriving from the ocean meets the Western Andes, rises, and cools. And then comes the rain. On the other side of these mountains lies one of the wettest and most biodiverse regions on the planet: the Chocó Biogeographic Region.

Here, water doesn’t only fall from the sky. It flows through countless rivers that cross the rainforest and, for many communities, also serve as pathways. For generations, Indigenous and Afro-Colombian communities have built ways of life deeply connected to the rivers, the rainforest, and the ocean. And this territory also has a voice.

Here, singing has also been a way to tell stories, celebrate, accompany one another, and preserve memory.

Now, look toward the ocean. Another traveler is arriving on these shores. Every year, humpback whales travel thousands of kilometers to reach Colombia’s Pacific coast, where they reproduce and raise their calves. Imagine an adult whale weighing tens of tons emerging from the water just off these shores.

Mountain. Rain. River. Rainforest. Ocean. Cultures. Here, everything is connected by water.

Keep going. The journey begins to climb once again.

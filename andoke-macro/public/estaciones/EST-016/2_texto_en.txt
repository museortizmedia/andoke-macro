# THE AMAZON AND THE ORIGIN OF ANDOKE

Look around you. The landscape has changed. The open spaces are behind us. Vegetation grows, climbs, and intertwines among towering trees, vines, and bromeliads.

We are in the Amazon. A territory of forest, water, and extraordinary diversity of life, inhabited and understood by many peoples for generations.

Now, look at the malocas. We are symbolically approaching Araracuara and the Caquetá River, an ancestral territory connected to the Andoque people, also known as “the people of the axe.” And here, this expedition touches our own story.

When Andoke’s founders learned about the relationship between the Andoque people, the axe, stone, and territory, they found an echo of it in this place. Look around you: terraces, pathways, and walls where stone also plays a central role. From that inspiration came our name: Andoke.

Let’s continue. The Tarapoto Lakes are home to one of the Amazon’s most iconic animals: the pink river dolphin. But this animal doesn’t only inhabit the rivers. It also inhabits stories. Amazonian oral traditions tell that, when night falls, the dolphin can transform into a man, arrive at celebrations, dance, and fall in love… and before dawn, return to the river.

The last malocas remind us of something essential: the Amazon is not an empty rainforest. It is the territory of Indigenous peoples, including the Tikuna, Cocama, and Yagua, whose lives and knowledge are deeply connected to the forest and its rivers.

And finally, before us: the Amazon River.

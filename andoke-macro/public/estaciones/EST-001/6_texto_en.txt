# WELCOME TO ANDOKE

Welcome to Andoke. My name is Valentina, and I’ll be accompanying you on this journey. But before we begin, I’d like to ask you to do something: stop for a moment. Don’t start walking just yet. Listen.

Can you recognize any of the sounds around you? Maybe a bird, leaves rustling, an insect… or simply the wind. Now, look around you. There are trees, plants, shade, and life everywhere. But when this story began, much of what you see today wasn’t here.

More than two decades ago, Alejandro del Llano and María del Carmen Tamayo came to this place with the idea of building a home close to nature. For the first few months, they lived here in a tent while they began planting and restoring a piece of land with very little vegetation. I was there too. I was only four years old. And as Andoke began to transform, I grew up alongside it.

With the plants came the insects. Then the butterflies appeared. After them came the birds. And little by little, this land began to fill with life. Over time, we realized that something extraordinary was happening: by restoring the conditions that allow life to thrive, nature was finding its way back.

What began as a place to live gradually found a greater purpose. First came a small butterfly house and, around it, new questions, new discoveries, and new ways of sharing this place with others. Over the years, Andoke became the place you are exploring today: a space dedicated to conservation, education, and science.

But we don’t want to tell you the whole story just yet. We want you to discover it as you walk. So, from this moment on, we have a small challenge for you: look closely. Because in nature, the most extraordinary stories aren’t always the biggest ones. Sometimes… they’re hidden on a leaf.

Welcome to Andoke. Let’s begin.

# GUATAVITA, ORINOQUIA, AND GUIANA SHIELD

We leave the Caribbean behind and return toward the interior of the country. The Eastern Andes still rise before us. Before the horizon opens up, let’s stop beside a lagoon that gave rise to one of the most famous stories ever told about the Americas: Guatavita.

For the Muisca people, it was a sacred place. Colonial chronicles described ceremonies and offerings made in its waters. Those accounts fueled an obsession among Europeans: El Dorado. The search for a land of extraordinary riches drove expeditions for centuries. But there is a paradox here. While some searched for the value of this land in gold… for those who inhabited it, its meaning was also rooted in their relationship with water, the mountains, and the sacred.

Now, look ahead. The mountains begin to disappear. The horizon grows wider. We are entering the Llanos — Colombia’s vast eastern plains. Here, water spreads across savannas, wetlands, and gallery forests toward the great Orinoco River basin. And the landscape changes with the seasons. During the rainy season, vast areas can become flooded. When the dry season arrives, the water recedes, and the landscape transforms once again.

These landscapes are home to capybaras, anteaters, deer, and one of the great cats of the Americas: the jaguar. But don’t try to find them all. Look toward the horizon. After walking among mountain ranges for much of this expedition, perhaps that vast open space is enough to understand just how much the landscape has changed.

Farther ahead, we encounter formations connected to a history far older than our own: the Guiana Shield. Some of its rocks belong to geological structures of immense age. And across them, different peoples also left human traces in the form of pictographs.

Now, notice how the vegetation begins to close in around you once again. The horizon disappears behind the trees. The humidity returns. We are entering the Amazon.

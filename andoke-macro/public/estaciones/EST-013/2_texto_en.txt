# CENTRAL ANDES, PÁRAMOS, AND DARIÉN

Just a few steps ago, we were listening to the Pacific Ocean. And now something extraordinary happens: without ever leaving Colombia, we can enter a completely different world simply by climbing a mountain. As altitude changes, so does the temperature. The vegetation changes. The animals change. And so does the way we inhabit the land.

Among these mountain slopes, we find one of the landscapes that has carried Colombia’s name around the world: the Coffee Cultural Landscape. Here, coffee found mountains, rainfall, and soils that helped transform a crop into part of the identity of an entire region. But let’s keep climbing.

The forest changes. The mist arrives. The páramo. The cold. And even higher… the peaks of Los Nevados National Natural Park. In a country that many people imagine as entirely tropical and warm, there are also peaks that have been covered by glaciers.

And above the Andes appears one of Colombia’s great symbols. Can you find it? The Andean condor. It is one of the largest flying birds in the world by wingspan. With its wings fully extended, it can reach nearly three meters across. And instead of constantly flapping its wings, it uses rising air currents to soar across great distances.

Now, continue north. The mountains still have stories to tell. Antioquia appears, with its landscapes of mountains and reservoirs. Farther ahead, toward the northwestern edge of the country, we reach one of the continent’s most extraordinary natural frontiers: the Darién. A vast region of rainforests and wetlands between Colombia and Panama.

And here, something remarkable happens: the Pan-American Highway stretches for thousands of kilometers across the continent… but it is interrupted at the Darién. The rainforest breaks the road. And as we continue toward the Caribbean, remember this image: in Colombia, sometimes geography still has the final say.

# BUTTERFLY BREEDING LABORATORY

Look around you. There are eggs, caterpillars, chrysalises, and butterflies hidden throughout this space. Some are alive; others are part of the exhibit. Can you identify all four stages?

In front of you is Andoke’s Butterfly Breeding Laboratory. And to discover what happens here, we invite you to try something different: for the next few minutes, imagine that you, too, are about to become a butterfly.

Look for the first stage: the egg. The female butterfly lays her eggs on specific plants known as host plants, because once the caterpillar hatches, that plant will become its food. And here, something remarkable happens: butterflies have chemical receptors on their feet that help them recognize plants when they land on them. You could say they “taste” the world with their feet. Choosing the right plant can determine the survival of the next generation. Great changes don’t always start out big.

Now let's look at the caterpillar. A caterpillar emerges from the egg with one essential mission: to eat and grow. And it grows so much that, several times during this stage, its skin becomes too tight and must be shed and replaced with a new one. Take a closer look. It has no wings. It cannot fly. And at first glance, nothing seems to reveal the butterfly it will eventually become. And yet, that extraordinary transformation is already underway.

Once the caterpillar has finished growing, it stops feeding and forms a pupa, which in many butterflies is called a chrysalis. A chrysalis is not simply a sleeping caterpillar. Inside, a profound biological reorganization is taking place: tissues are transformed, and the structures of the adult butterfly develop. On the outside, it may seem as though nothing is happening. Inside, a revolution is underway.

When the butterfly emerges, its wings are still soft and folded. It must expand them and wait until they are ready before it can fly.

Egg. Caterpillar. Chrysalis. Butterfly. They are not four different animals. They are one life passing through four different forms. This laboratory exists to support that cycle and to help us understand the relationships that make it possible: the right plants, water, climate, and habitat.

Now you know where the story begins. But we don’t want to explain the final part here. We want you to experience it. Continue ahead and enter the butterfly house. It’s time to fly.
